01

Your library, without the spreadsheet

Add a book by searching Google Books, mark it favourite, owned, reading, or finished, and keep a running shelf you can filter and sort. Save the quotes that stick with you, each one tied to where it came from.

  • Search and add books straight from Google Books — cover, author, and description come along for free
  • Filter your shelf by read, owned, or genre, and pin favourites to the top
  • Keep a running collection of quotes, each with its source, searchable whenever you want to find one again
02

Notebooks for whatever doesn't fit anywhere else

Notebooks group your notes so a travel journal and a recipe box don't turn into one long, unsorted list. And whenever a book, contact, reminder, or quote deserves a quick aside, a remark attaches right to it.

  • Organise notes into notebooks with rich text — headings, lists, tables, images, whatever the entry needs
  • Attach a short remark to any book, contact, reminder, or quote, right where you're already looking at it
  • Search across notebooks and notes to find the one page you half-remember writing
03

People and the things you meant to do

Keep contacts with the details you actually need — address, birthday, how to reach them — and a running list of reminders, grouped and recurring, so nothing important slips past you.

  • Contacts show upcoming birthdays a week out, so you're never caught off guard
  • Group reminders and drag them between groups as plans change
  • Set reminders to repeat by day, week, month, or year — no need to re-add the same one every time
04

A place to write down where you're headed

One page per horizon — this month, one year, five years, ten years — for the kind of thinking that gets lost in a to-do app. It's the first thing your overview page reminds you of when you sit down.

  • Write freely in each horizon; empty ones just stay out of the way
  • Your overview page opens with a daily quote, what you're reading, what's due today, and your vision — one screen, nothing to dig for
  • Everything here is yours alone — Commonplace is a personal account, not a shared workspace
