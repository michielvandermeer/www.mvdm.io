01

Run your AI management system against ISO 42001

Compliance has the ISO 42001:2023 catalog. It holds the management system requirements in clauses 4 to 10 and the Annex A controls A.2 to A.10.

Clause 5.2 asks for an AI policy, and Annex A control A.2.2 says what it must do. The catalog gives A.2.2 as "Document an AI policy that is approved by management, sets the organisation's approach to developing or using AI systems, and is aligned with business objectives and applicable obligations."

The catalog also includes the impact assessment requirements, clause 6.1.4 "AI system impact assessment" and Annex A controls A.5.2 and A.5.3. You link evidence, findings and register records to the requirements they meet.

The Annex A controls are optional requirements. On the Applicability page of ISO 42001 you mark which of them apply to your organisation. For ISO standards the page is labelled SoA, and it is your Statement of Applicability.

02

Your frameworks overlap. Your work doesn't have to.

Crosswalk mappings link related requirements across the frameworks you adopt, and Compliance crosswalks ISO 42001 to ISO 27001. Evidence, findings and register records can then meet requirements in both standards.

Awareness is one example. The crosswalk marks ISO 27001 clause 7.3 and ISO 42001 clause 7.3 as equivalent, so your awareness evidence can meet both. Policy is a partial match. ISO 42001 clause 5.2 asks for an AI policy, so it only partly matches ISO 27001 clause 5.2 on the information security policy.

If you also adopt the EU AI Act, shared crosswalk topics show where the frameworks overlap. Transparency is one of them, with ISO 42001 A.8.2 and EU AI Act Art. 50.

An audit still covers one standard, so ISO 42001 gets its own audit. A supplier review covers one supplier, and it does not span frameworks either.

03

Shared registers, risks and review tasks

Compliance has registers for policies, risks, assets, suppliers, evidence and findings, among others. The registers are shared between the frameworks you adopt, so ISO 42001 and ISO 27001 use the same records.

Compliance scores each risk on a 5×5 likelihood and impact matrix. Treatment actions lower the residual risk score.

Compliance creates recurring review tasks for policies, risks, assets and requirements on schedule. Every task has an owner and a due date, and all tasks appear in one inbox.

Compliance also has an AI assistant on every page. It runs on AI credits, billed in USD, and it is not part of the trial unless you buy AI credits.

An auditor certifies your organisation against ISO 42001. Compliance is where you run the AI management system and keep the evidence, findings and records the auditor asks to see.
