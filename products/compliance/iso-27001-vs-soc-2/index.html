ISO 27001 vs SOC 2: start with the one your customers ask for

ISO 27001 is an international standard for an information security management system (ISMS). A certification body audits your ISMS against it and issues a certificate. SOC 2 is a US attestation from the AICPA. A CPA firm examines your controls against the Trust Services Criteria and writes a report.

If most of your customers are in the US and ask for a SOC 2 report, start with SOC 2. In most other cases, start with ISO 27001. The two share many security topics, such as access control, change management and risk assessment, so the second one builds on the work you did for the first. Each one still has its own audit.

This page covers what each one is, how they differ, which one to start with and how to run both.

01

What ISO 27001 is

ISO/IEC 27001 is published by ISO and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). It sets the requirements for an ISMS: how you decide which information security risks matter, how you treat them and how you keep the system working.

Clauses 4 to 10 cover the management system itself, such as context, leadership, risk assessment, internal audit and management review. Annex A of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 lists 93 controls. You decide which ones apply to you and record that in a Statement of Applicability.

Certification is optional. If you want a certificate, a certification body audits you. ISO notes that a certificate from an accredited certification body can give customers more confidence. Accredited certification runs in a three-year cycle, with an initial audit, surveillance audits in the first and second year and a recertification audit in the third.

02

What SOC 2 is

SOC 2 comes from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA). It is an examination of the controls at a service organization, such as a SaaS company, against the Trust Services Criteria.

The criteria are grouped into five categories: Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy. Every SOC 2 report covers Security, through the Common Criteria. You choose which of the other categories to add to your scope.

A licensed CPA firm performs the examination and issues the report. There are two types:

  • A Type 1 report looks at whether your controls are suitably designed, as of one date.
  • A Type 2 report also tests whether your controls operated effectively throughout a period, and it describes the auditor's tests and results.

SOC 2 is an attestation report, not a certification. The report is meant for your customers and other specified parties.

03

The differences that matter when you choose

  • ISO 27001 ends in a certificate. SOC 2 ends in a report with the auditor's opinion.
  • A certification body audits ISO 27001. A CPA firm examines your controls for SOC 2.
  • ISO 27001 asks for a full management system plus the Annex A controls that apply to you. SOC 2 measures your controls against the Trust Services Criteria for the categories in your scope.
  • ISO 27001 is an international standard. SOC 2 is a US framework, so it comes up most with US customers.

Neither one is better in general. The right one is the one your customers accept.

04

Which one to start with

Look at the security questionnaires and contract terms you get from customers and prospects. They tell you which one to do first.

  • If most of your customers are in the US and ask for a SOC 2 report by name, start with SOC 2.
  • If your customers are in Europe or spread over several regions, start with ISO 27001.
  • If nobody has asked yet, ISO 27001 is a reasonable default, because it is an international standard.

Some customers accept either one. Others ask for one by name. When a large customer asks for the other one later, you add it.

05

Doing both

If your customers ask for both, you keep one ISMS and run two audits: an ISO 27001 audit by a certification body and a SOC 2 examination by a CPA firm. One audit does not cover the other standard.

Much of the work behind the two audits is shared. An access control policy, a risk assessment or a change management procedure is useful for both, and the evidence you keep for it can be evidence for both. Each auditor decides what to accept as evidence for their own standard.

Questions about ISO 27001 and SOC 2

What is the difference between ISO 27001 and SOC 2?

ISO 27001 is an international standard for an information security management system. A certification body audits you against it and issues a certificate. SOC 2 is a US attestation from the AICPA. A CPA firm examines your controls against the Trust Services Criteria and issues a report, either Type 1 or Type 2.

Should I start with ISO 27001 or SOC 2?

Start with the one your customers ask for. If most of your customers are in the US and ask for a SOC 2 report, start with SOC 2. If your customers are in Europe or spread over several regions, or nobody has asked yet, start with ISO 27001.

Is SOC 2 required in Europe?

No. SOC 2 is a voluntary attestation from a US professional body, not a legal requirement. Some customers ask for a SOC 2 report in their contracts or security questionnaires, often customers in the US. Many European customers ask for ISO 27001 instead.

Can ISO 27001 replace SOC 2?

Only if your customer accepts it. Some customers accept an ISO 27001 certificate instead of a SOC 2 report, and others ask for SOC 2 by name. Ask the customer before you plan the audit.

How much do ISO 27001 and SOC 2 overlap?

The two share many security topics. In Compliance, the crosswalk marks ISO 27001 A.5.15 on access control as Equivalent to SOC 2 CC6.1, and ISO 27001 A.8.32 on change management as Equivalent to SOC 2 CC8.1. They still differ. ISO 27001 asks for a full management system, and SOC 2 can include categories such as Privacy and Processing Integrity. Each one has its own audit.
