SOC 2 vs GDPR: an attestation report and a law

SOC 2 is a US attestation from the AICPA. A CPA firm examines your controls against the Trust Services Criteria and writes a report. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is law on personal data. A SOC 2 report does not by itself show that you meet the GDPR.

This page covers what each one is, how they differ, where to start and how to run both. Start by working out which GDPR requirements apply to your organisation. A SOC 2 report gives your customers the auditor's opinion on your controls, but your GDPR obligations still need their own assessment.

01

What SOC 2 is

SOC 2 comes from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA). It is an examination of the controls at a service organization, such as a SaaS company, against the Trust Services Criteria.

The criteria are grouped into five categories: Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy. Every SOC 2 report covers Security, through the Common Criteria. You choose which of the other categories to add to your scope.

A licensed CPA firm performs the examination and issues the report, either Type 1 or Type 2. SOC 2 is an attestation report, not a certification.

02

What the GDPR is

The EU GDPR sets requirements for handling personal data. These include principles under Art. 5, lawful processing under Art. 6, data subject rights under Art. 12 to 21, processors under Art. 28, security of processing under Art. 32 and personal data breaches under Art. 33.

Other requirements apply in particular situations, such as a data protection impact assessment under Art. 35 or a data protection officer under Art. 37 to 39. You and your legal adviser decide what applies to your organisation.

The UK GDPR is the GDPR as kept in UK law after Brexit, read together with the Data Protection Act 2018. EU GDPR and UK GDPR are separate frameworks in Compliance. You adopt the one that applies to you, or both.

03

The differences that matter

  • SOC 2 is a voluntary attestation that some customers ask for. The GDPR is law where it applies to you.
  • SOC 2 measures your controls against the Trust Services Criteria for the categories in your scope. The GDPR covers more than security, such as lawful processing, data subject rights and processor obligations.
  • A SOC 2 report gives the auditor's opinion on your controls. It does not by itself show that your processing meets the GDPR.
  • Privacy is an optional category in SOC 2. In Compliance, the closest links to the GDPR are with the Privacy criteria, so they matter only if your SOC 2 examination includes Privacy.
04

Where to start

Start with the GDPR requirements that apply to your organisation, because they are legal obligations. Work through them with your legal adviser rather than treating a SOC 2 report as the answer.

If your customers ask for a SOC 2 report, plan the examination as well. SOC 2 comes up most with US customers. If you already have a SOC 2 report, reuse the records that can support GDPR requirements and check what is still open.

05

Doing both

You can run SOC 2 and GDPR work from the same records where requirements are related. An access control policy and its supporting evidence can be evidence for requirements in both. Evidence and findings can meet requirements in several frameworks.

An audit still covers one standard. The CPA firm decides what to accept for your SOC 2 examination. You and your legal adviser still assess what the GDPR asks of your organisation. Shared records do not turn a SOC 2 report into a GDPR compliance decision.

06

SOC 2 and the GDPR in Compliance

Compliance by mvdmio has catalogs for SOC 2, EU GDPR and UK GDPR. Crosswalk mappings link related requirements across the frameworks you adopt. Each link has an app label: Equivalent, Partial or Related.

The closest links between the GDPR and SOC 2 are with the SOC 2 Privacy criteria.

  • The crosswalk marks the data subject rights in GDPR Art. 15 to 18, Art. 20 and Art. 21 as Equivalent to SOC 2 P5.1 and P5.2.
  • It marks GDPR Art. 13 and 14 as Equivalent to P1.1.
  • It marks GDPR Art. 5(1)(e) on storage limitation as Equivalent to P4.2 and P4.3.

The Privacy criteria are optional in SOC 2, so these links matter only if your SOC 2 examination includes Privacy.

One access control record can meet requirements in SOC 2 CC6.1, ISO 27001 A.5.15 and GDPR Art. 32. The crosswalk marks CC6.1 as Equivalent to A.5.15. It marks Art. 32 as Related to both.

Each framework shows what is satisfied and what is still open. Proposed coverage suggests coverage from the crosswalk once you cover a source requirement. You accept or dismiss each suggestion. Compliance applies none of them on its own.

Questions about SOC 2 and the GDPR

What is the difference between SOC 2 and the GDPR?

SOC 2 is a US attestation from the AICPA. A CPA firm examines your controls against the Trust Services Criteria and issues a report, either Type 1 or Type 2. The GDPR is law on personal data, with requirements that include lawful processing, data subject rights, processors and security of processing.

Does a SOC 2 report show GDPR compliance?

Not by itself. The report gives the auditor's opinion on your controls against the Trust Services Criteria in your scope. GDPR obligations also cover topics such as lawful processing and data subject rights. Work on your SOC 2 controls can be evidence for GDPR requirements, but you and your legal adviser still assess what the GDPR asks of you. This answer is not legal advice.

Should I start with SOC 2 or the GDPR?

Start with the GDPR requirements that apply to your organisation, because they are legal obligations. Plan a SOC 2 examination if your customers ask for a SOC 2 report, often customers in the US. If you already have a SOC 2 report, reuse the records that can support GDPR requirements and check what is still open.

Can I reuse SOC 2 work for the GDPR?

Partly. The crosswalk marks GDPR Art. 15 to 18, Art. 20 and Art. 21 as Equivalent to the SOC 2 Privacy criteria P5.1 and P5.2, which matter only if your SOC 2 examination includes Privacy. One access control record can also meet requirements in CC6.1 and Art. 32, and the crosswalk marks Art. 32 as Related to CC6.1. Your GDPR obligations still need their own assessment.

Are EU GDPR and UK GDPR the same framework in Compliance?

No. You adopt the one that applies to you, or both. The EU GDPR and the UK GDPR are separate frameworks, and each costs €199 per framework per year, excl. VAT. Each includes $5 of AI credit per year, billed in USD, and users are unlimited. You pay for each framework you adopt separately.
