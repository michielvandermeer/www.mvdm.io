ISO 42001 vs EU AI Act: a voluntary standard and a law

ISO/IEC 42001 is an international standard for an AI management system. You choose to adopt it, and a certification body can certify your organisation against it. The EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, is EU law. What it asks of you depends on your role, such as provider or deployer, and on the AI systems you build or use.

So you don't choose one over the other. Work out first which AI Act obligations apply to you. ISO 42001 is a way to run your AI governance as a management system, and several of its topics, such as transparency, human oversight and competence, relate to AI Act articles. An ISO 42001 certificate does not show that you meet the AI Act.

This page covers what each one is, how they differ, where to start and how to run both.

01

What ISO 42001 is

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is published by ISO and the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). It sets requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an AI management system. It is meant for organisations that develop, provide or use AI-based products or services.

Clauses 4 to 10 cover the management system itself. Clause 5.2 asks for an AI policy, and clause 6.1.4 asks for an AI system impact assessment. The Annex A controls A.2 to A.10 are optional requirements. You decide which ones apply to you and record that in a Statement of Applicability.

ISO 42001 is voluntary. If you want a certificate, a certification body audits your AI management system and issues it.

02

What the EU AI Act is

The EU AI Act is Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It applies to providers that place AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the EU market, wherever they are established, and to deployers of AI systems in the EU. It also applies to providers and deployers outside the EU when the output of their AI system is used in the EU, and to importers and distributors.

The Regulation defines the roles:

  • A provider develops an AI system or a general-purpose AI model, or has one developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name or trademark.
  • A deployer uses an AI system under its authority, except for personal, non-professional use.

What you must do depends on that role and on the kind of AI system. The Regulation has rules on AI literacy (Art. 4), prohibited AI practices (Art. 5), high-risk AI systems, with obligations for providers (Art. 16) and deployers (Art. 26), transparency for certain AI systems (Art. 50) and providers of general-purpose AI models (Art. 53 and Art. 55).

Providers of high-risk AI systems go through a conformity assessment before they place a system on the market (Art. 43). For some high-risk AI systems a notified body takes part. For others the provider does it through internal control.

The AI Act applies in stages, so different rules start on different dates.

03

The differences that matter

  • ISO 42001 is a voluntary standard. The AI Act is law for everyone in its scope.
  • ISO 42001 asks for a management system for all your AI work. The AI Act sets obligations per role and per kind of AI system.
  • ISO 42001 can end in a certificate from a certification body. Under the AI Act, providers of high-risk AI systems go through a conformity assessment, sometimes with a notified body.
  • ISO 42001 is an international standard. The AI Act covers AI placed on the EU market or used in the EU, including by organisations outside the EU.

An ISO 42001 certificate shows that a certification body audited your AI management system against ISO 42001. It does not show that you meet the AI Act.

04

Where to start

Start with the AI Act, because it is law where it applies to you. List the AI systems you build and use, work out your role for each one and check which rules apply. That is a legal question, so take your own legal advice.

  • If you build AI systems or general-purpose AI models for the EU market, start with your obligations as a provider.
  • If you use AI systems in your work in the EU, start with your obligations as a deployer.
  • If you want a certificate that shows how you govern AI, add ISO 42001. It is voluntary, so the choice is yours.
  • If you already run ISO 27001, ISO 42001 has management system clauses 4 to 10 too, so it builds on the system you have.

ISO 42001 can also be the place where you organise the AI Act work, with policies, risks, impact assessments and evidence in one management system. That doesn't replace any AI Act obligation.

05

Doing both

If you do both, the AI Act obligations for your role stay yours, whatever you certify. ISO 42001 gets its own audit by a certification body.

Some of the work is useful for both. A transparency procedure, human oversight measures or a competence plan can be evidence for requirements in both. Your ISO 42001 auditor decides what to accept for ISO 42001. For the AI Act, the Regulation sets what your role needs, and where a notified body takes part, it makes its own assessment.

ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act in Compliance

Compliance by mvdmio has catalogs for ISO 42001:2023 and the EU AI Act. The EU AI Act catalog holds requirements for Art. 4, Art. 5, Art. 6(1), Art. 16, Art. 26, Art. 50, Art. 53 and Art. 55. It marks Art. 4 and Art. 5 as mandatory and the others as optional. On the Applicability page you mark which requirements apply to your organisation. Compliance does not decide whether your AI system is high-risk. You make that call, with your own legal advice.

Crosswalk mappings link related requirements across the frameworks you adopt, and the crosswalk marks each link as Equivalent, Partial or Related.

  • Transparency. The crosswalk marks ISO 42001 control A.8.2 as Equivalent to AI Act Art. 50.
  • Human oversight. It marks ISO 42001 controls A.9.2, A.9.3 and A.9.4 as Related to AI Act Art. 26.
  • Lifecycle. It marks ISO 42001 clauses 8.1, 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4 as Related to AI Act Art. 16.
  • AI literacy. It marks AI Act Art. 4 as Related to ISO 42001 clause 7.2 on competence.

AI Act requirements outside these topics have no crosswalk link to ISO 42001. Where there is a link, evidence, findings and register records can meet requirements in both frameworks. Each framework shows what is satisfied and what is still open.

Questions about ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act

What is the difference between ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act?

ISO 42001 is a voluntary international standard for an AI management system, and a certification body can certify you against it. The EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, is EU law. Its obligations depend on your role, such as provider or deployer, and on the kind of AI system.

Does an ISO 42001 certificate mean I meet the EU AI Act?

No. An ISO 42001 certificate shows that a certification body audited your AI management system against ISO 42001. The AI Act has its own obligations for your role and your AI systems. Some ISO 42001 work can be evidence for AI Act requirements, but you still check each obligation yourself. This answer is not legal advice.

Should I start with ISO 42001 or the EU AI Act?

Start with the AI Act, because it is law where it applies to you. Work out your role and which rules apply to your AI systems. Add ISO 42001 if you want a certificate that shows how you govern AI.

Does the EU AI Act apply to companies outside the EU?

It can. The AI Act applies to providers that place AI systems or general-purpose AI models on the EU market, wherever they are established. It also applies to providers and deployers outside the EU when the output of their AI system is used in the EU. This answer is not legal advice.

How much do ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act overlap?

They share topics such as transparency, human oversight, lifecycle and AI literacy. In Compliance, the crosswalk marks ISO 42001 control A.8.2 as Equivalent to AI Act Art. 50 on transparency, and ISO 42001 controls A.9.2, A.9.3 and A.9.4 as Related to Art. 26. AI Act requirements outside these topics have no crosswalk link to ISO 42001.
