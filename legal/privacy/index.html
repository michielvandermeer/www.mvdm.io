  • mvdm.io Commonplace Journal — the browser’s location, or the IP address when the browser gives none, is used only to name the place where a Journal entry is written. Only that place name is kept.

5. Analytics

mvdm.io Statistics captures first-party request analytics for the other applications: the signed-in user’s name, or their email address where no name is available; the full URL requested, including its query string; timing; and tags. This means your email address can end up standing in for your name in that record. mvdm.io Commonplace sends nothing to Statistics. This is our own instrumentation, not a third-party advertising tracker.

mvdm.io Statistics also counts Page views on the marketing site and on the Demos at demo.mvdm.io. When a page is shown, a small script from statistics.mvdm.io sends one Page view. The script sets no cookie, writes nothing to your device, and uses no visitor identifier, so we cannot recognise you when you come back. A Page view holds the time, the page’s hostname and path without its query string, the hostname of the page that linked to it, the utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign of the page’s address, and a country. Statistics uses your IP address for two lookups, both in a database inside Statistics: the country, and whether the address is on a data-centre network. It then drops the address without storing it. A Page view from an address on a data-centre network is not kept. Cloudflare carries the request, as it does for every Product, and no analytics company receives it.

When you follow a link from one of these pages to another mvdm.io page, the script can add a via value to that link’s address. It names the site or campaign that brought you, which many people share, so it identifies no one. The next page that runs the script removes it from the address bar. If that link takes you on to register an account, the account keeps the via value, as section 2 says.

A customer can use Statistics on its own website in the same way. For those Page views the customer decides what is measured and is the controller, and mvdm.io is its processor under the Data Processing Agreement.

mvdm.io also uses Statistics inside its own signed-in Products, starting with Compliance, to record App views and Clicks. When you open a page, or click a button, link, or labelled control, a small script from statistics.mvdm.io sends one App view or one Click. An App view holds the time, the user id, the id of the user’s organisation, the page’s address, including its query string, and its title, the browser, the OS, the device type, the language, the screen size, and a country. A Click holds the same, plus the clicked element and the visible text of a button, link, or labelled control. Neither holds an IP address, and a Click never holds what a person typed. The text of a Click on anything that shows a customer’s record content is masked, so record content is never kept. The script sets no cookie and writes nothing to your device. For these App views and Clicks mvdm.io is the controller. The next paragraph is about a customer’s own application, not about mvdm.io’s Products.

A customer’s application can send Statistics Requests, App views, and Clicks. For a Request, Statistics stores the time, the requested address including its query string, the user id, the id of the user’s organisation, the user name, the processing time, and Tags. A Request holds no IP address: Statistics receives the address of the request, not the address of your computer. For an App view, it stores the time, the user id, the id of the user’s organisation, the page’s address, including its query string, and its title, the browser, the OS, the device type, the language, the screen size, and a country. An App view holds no IP address. For a Click, it stores the time, the user id, the id of the user’s organisation, the page’s address, including its query string, and its title, the browser, the OS, the device type, the language, the screen size, and a country, plus the clicked element and the visible text of a button, link, or labelled control. A Click holds no IP address and never holds what a person typed. A customer’s application can also send Track events for a signed-in App user, from a person’s browser or from the customer’s own server. For a Track event, Statistics stores the time, the name, the user id, the id of the user’s organisation, and the Properties, such as a plan or an amount, that the customer’s application chooses. A Track event sent from a person’s browser also stores the page’s hostname and path. For each App user and each organisation an App user works for, Statistics also stores the name and the Fields, such as a role or a plan, that the customer’s own server sends. The customer chooses those Fields. Fields never come from a person’s browser. For these records and Fields the customer decides what is measured and is the controller, and mvdm.io is its processor under the Data Processing Agreement. The script that sends App views and Clicks sets no cookie and writes nothing to your device.

When you use the assistant in mvdm.io Compliance, mvdm.io Translation Tools, mvdm.io Commonplace or mvdm.io Sales, conversation content is processed so the feature can run (see the Data Processing Agreement for the processor role). A Platform Administrator of mvdm.io can read those assistant conversations for support and quality. Import conversations used by Compliance’s import feature are likewise visible to Platform Administrators for debugging.

mvdm.io Commonplace

For Commonplace, mvdm.io processes the content of your journal as controller. When you use the Assistant, SpaceX Super Intelligence LLC (xAI) receives your messages, your attachments, and the records the Assistant reads to answer you. When the Assistant searches the web, the search providers xAI uses receive its search queries.

That content goes to the United States. No adequacy decision covers xAI, so the transfer rests on the standard contractual clauses in xAI’s data processing agreement.

At mvdm.io a conversation stays until you delete it, closed-licence retention removes it, or you withdraw from your Commonplace subscription, which deletes it at once (see section 8). At xAI it stays for at most 30 days, and longer only for abuse or legal holds. Under their published terms, these providers do not use that content to train models.

A Platform Administrator of mvdm.io reads Assistant conversations for support and quality, on mvdm.io’s legitimate interest in running the service named in section 3. We may also analyse exported conversations with Claude Code, which makes Anthropic a recipient of them. Anthropic is not on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework List. Its transfers rest on adequacy decisions and the standard contractual clauses, and Anthropic Ireland, Limited is its entity in the European Economic Area.

If your journal mentions other people, they get the same information under Article 14 of the GDPR.

8. Retention

Commission records and self-billing invoices are kept for 7 years, as Dutch tax law requires. Sign-in and billing records are kept while the account is active and for as long as needed for security, accounting, and legal obligations. The Audit Log is the exception. It records each sign-in and each change to who has access to an account, with the IP address and browser it came from. We delete each Audit Log entry 365 days after it happened. Product data after a licence ends follows closed-licence retention: when a Product’s licence ends, we keep that account’s data for that Product for 365 days from the licence end date. If you obtain a new current licence for that Product within those 365 days, the countdown stops and the data remains. If you do not, we delete that account’s data for that Product after 365 days. Your sign-in account and users are not deleted by that wipe. There is one exception. When you withdraw from a Commonplace subscription, we delete that account’s Commonplace data at once. What is left on the account’s Commonplace Assistant balance still follows the 365-day rule. Data we delete this way, and data we delete after you withdraw from a Commonplace purchase, leave every Backup within 30 days. Apart from that exception, the closed-licence retention rule is the same for every Product, as described in the Terms of Service and the Data Processing Agreement. Page views on the marketing site and the Demos hold nothing that identifies you, so they have no fixed retention period, and we keep them until we delete them from Statistics. A Lead from one of our own Forms who never replies to any of our emails is deleted two years after they signed up. A Prospect who never replies to any of my emails is deleted two years after my first email. A Prospect who asks me to stop is deleted at once, apart from their email address, which I keep on a list of addresses I must not email for as long as I email people without them signing up.

I found your email address on a public page, such as your organisation’s website, and I keep that page and the words on it that show your address, so I can show where I found it. I use this only to email you about compliance. Section 7 names who else receives it. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in offering mvdm.io’s Products to businesses. If you never reply to any of my emails, I delete your record two years after my first email. Ask me to stop at michiel@mvdm.io and I will stop at once and delete your record. I keep only your email address, on a list of addresses I must not email, so that I never email you again.
